## Policy, Legal and Privacy Constraints

*This section is research, not legal advice. Where it says whether a rule "applies" to Campus Recall, that is the researcher's reading of public text. A lawyer, or Meta's own review of the WhatsApp Business Account, would have the final say.*

### 1. Meta's "AI Providers" restriction (effective 15 January 2026)

**The clause as it stands now.** According to the current [Meta Terms for WhatsApp Business Platform](https://www.facebook.com/legal/Meta-Terms-for-WhatsApp-Business-Platform) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary), last modified 23 September 2026, Section 4.7 says that "providers and developers of artificial intelligence or machine learning technologies", which it defines as including large language models, generative AI platforms and general-purpose AI assistants "as determined by Meta in its sole discretion", are "strictly prohibited from accessing or using the WhatsApp Business Platform, whether directly or indirectly", "when such technologies are the primary (rather than incidental or ancillary) functionality being made available for use". The same section has three carve-outs. First, such technologies "may be made available to businesses in certain countries", listed on a separate developer page. Second, a business may hire an AI provider as its Solution Provider. Third, a business may fine-tune a model "for your exclusive use". Section 4.7 also bans letting "WhatsApp Business Platform Data" be used "to create, develop, train, or improve any machine learning or artificial intelligence systems". The old URL, whatsapp.com/legal/business-solution-terms, now redirects (301) to this Meta-hosted page. The term "Business Solution Terms" is still used in the regulators' documents.

**Dates and the main targets.** The effective date and the main targets are well documented. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/18/whatssapp-changes-its-terms-to-bar-general-purpose-chatbots-from-its-platform) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Established) reported the change in October 2025, and [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/10/20/whatsapp-bans-ai-chatbots-from-using-its-business-api/) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Established) reported that the terms took effect on 15 January 2026 and that OpenAI, Perplexity, Luzia and Poke were affected. The [European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1276/IP_26_1276_EN.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Primary) says the policy was introduced on 15 October 2025 for new users. Meta's own explanation, quoted by TechCrunch, was that "AI chatbots on our Business API put a strain on our systems". Meta also argued that the Business API is for businesses serving their customers, not for distributing chatbots.

**Status in late 2026: European Union / EEA.** According to the Commission's [IP/26/1276 press release](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1276/IP_26_1276_EN.pdf) (Brussels, 9 June 2026; accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Primary), the timeline was as follows:

- December 2025: the Commission opened case AT.41034.
- February 2026: it issued a Statement of Objections.
- April 2026: it issued a supplementary Statement of Objections.
- 9 June 2026: it ordered Meta to "re-instate access for third-party general-purpose AI assistants to the WhatsApp for Business API under the same terms and conditions that were in place before 15 October 2025, when such access was notably free of charge". Meta had five working days to comply, and the measures last until a final decision.

The Commission also found that Meta's 4 March 2026 revision, which let general-purpose assistants back in but charged a fee, was "in practice equivalent to the previous access ban". This is only the second interim-measures decision under Regulation 1/2003; the first was against Broadcom in 2009. [Houthoff](https://www.houthoff.com/insights/news/a-rare-show-offorce-commission-deploys-interim-measures-against-meta-to-safeguard-ai-competition/) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Established) confirms the same timeline. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/09/eu-interim-measures-meta-whatsapp-ai-policy-antritrust.html) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Established) reported the February step as the Commission's stated intention, and quoted Meta as saying the Commission "incorrectly assumes the WhatsApp Business API is a key distribution channel for these chatbots". [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320553/20260715/eu-antitrust-order-reopens-whatsapp-chatgpt-setting-precedent-ai-markets.htm) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: Medium, tier: Established, lower-quality outlet) reports that the order covers the EEA, that ChatGPT came back on WhatsApp in the EU from about 13 July 2026, and that Meta has appealed without suspending the order. No second source confirmed the appeal or the return date.

**Status in late 2026: Italy.** According to the [AGCM press release A576](https://en.agcm.it/en/media/press-releases/2025/12/A576) (Rome, 24 December 2025; accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Primary), the Italian authority ordered Meta Platforms Inc., Meta Platforms Ireland, WhatsApp Ireland and Facebook Italy to "immediately suspend the WhatsApp Business Solution Terms" as far as their effects in Italy are concerned. Its abuse-of-dominance investigation, opened in July 2025, is still running. [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/italy-opens-antitrust-probe-into-meta-over-whatsapp-ai-chatbot-exclusion/) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: Low, tier: Low) gives 31 December 2026 as the deadline for closing the investigation. No final AGCM decision was found.

**Status in late 2026: Brazil.** [TechCrunch (13 Jan 2026)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/13/brazil-orders-meta-to-suspend-policy-banning-third-party-ai-chatbots-from-whatsapp) and [TechCrunch (15 Jan 2026)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/15/after-italy-whatsapp-excludes-brazil-from-rival-chatbot-ban) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Established) report that CADE ordered the policy suspended and opened an investigation, and that WhatsApp then exempted Brazilian (+55) numbers. [TechCrunch (28 Jan 2026)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/28/whatsapp-will-now-charge-ai-chatbots-to-operate-in-italy/) then reported that a Brazilian court had overturned the preliminary order. [TechCrunch (6 Mar 2026)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/06/after-europe-whatsapp-will-let-rival-ai-companies-offer-chatbots-in-brazil/) reported that CADE rejected Meta's appeal, and that Meta now allows AI providers in Brazil for USD 0.0625 per non-template message from 11 March 2026.

**Status in late 2026: Meta's country list.** Meta's [AI Providers pricing page](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/ai-providers) (last updated 15 October 2026; accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary) lists the markets where AI providers are allowed:

- Italy (+39): charged 16 Feb to 12 May 2026, no charge from 13 May 2026.
- Brazil (+55): charged from 11 March 2026, with no end date.
- Türkiye (+90): no charge.
- About 29 EU/EEA countries: charged 11 March to 12 May 2026, no charge from 13 May 2026.

India is not on the list. The page says the policy does not change how Meta charges "all other businesses". It also says messages from AI providers appear in webhooks with the pricing category `general_purpose_ai`.

**Status in late 2026: India (CCI).** No sources were found for any Competition Commission of India complaint, investigation or order about the WhatsApp AI-provider restriction. Searches turned up only Indian vendor blogs restating the global rule. On the evidence found, the restriction applies in full to Indian (+91) numbers.

**Would a narrow save-and-search bot fall inside the ban?** This is analysis, not a sourced fact. Campus Recall's bot stores files and returns matching saved items. It does not generate answers and it is not an open-ended assistant. It does use machine-learning models: OCR, embeddings and ranking. Read literally, Section 4.7 is triggered when AI/ML technology is "the primary (rather than incidental or ancillary) functionality being made available". The product here is storage and retrieval of the student's own items, and the ML is the means of ranking results. That points to "ancillary", so the bot is probably outside the ban. Three things work against that reading. First, the definition is "as determined by Meta in its sole discretion", so Meta, not the team, decides. Second, the clause names "machine learning technologies" broadly, not only LLMs. Third, a bot that accepts any free-text query and searches anything a student has ever saved could look like a general "second brain" assistant to a reviewer.

The Established and vendor sources describe the ban as aimed at general-purpose assistants, with business bots for support and task-specific flows left alone. See [respond.io](https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-general-purpose-chatbots-ban) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: Low, tier: Low, vendor blog) and the [Meta AI Providers page](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/ai-providers). This suggests the risk is low, but it is not zero.

Practical steps that would strengthen the "ancillary" reading:

- Describe the bot in its WhatsApp profile and privacy notice as a file locker / bookmark service.
- Answer only with saved items, never with generated text.
- Do not add an LLM answer feature.
- Never train or fine-tune on WhatsApp-received content. Section 4.7 forbids training any model on "WhatsApp Business Platform Data" other than a model for exclusive use, and the project's pinned, pre-trained models are compatible with that.

### 2. WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy requirements

According to the [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) (last updated 23 September 2026; accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary), the main rules are these.

**Opt-in.** A business may contact a person only if "they have given you their mobile phone number or username" and it has "opt-in permission from the recipient confirming that they wish to receive subsequent messages". A student who messages the bot first opens the 24-hour customer-service window, and the bot can reply freely inside it. Any message the bot sends on its own later (for example a "your file is ready" alert after 24 hours) needs explicit opt-in and an approved template.

**Opt-out.** The business must "respect all requests (either on or off WhatsApp) by a person to block, discontinue, or otherwise opt out".

**Privacy policy.** The business must obtain the necessary notices and consents, "including maintaining a published privacy policy". In practice Meta also asks for a privacy-policy URL in the app and business profile setup. That last point is our understanding and was not separately verified for this report.

**Human escalation.** "You may use automation when responding during the 24-hour window, but must also have available prompt, clear, and direct escalation paths". The examples given include in-chat transfer to a human agent. For a student project, a "reply HELP to reach the team" path, or a listed email address, is the minimum reading.

**Bot disclosure.** The primary policy text found has no explicit requirement to say "you are talking to a bot". See the conflicts note below.

**Prohibited content and uses.** The policy bans offensive content, "for example sexually explicit materials or nudity", and using WhatsApp "to facilitate or organize criminal activity". It also bars asking people to share "full length individual payment card numbers, financial account numbers, personal ID card numbers, or other sensitive identifiers". Students will upload ID cards, fee receipts and similar documents, so the bot should not ask for them and should warn against sending them.

**Data use.** Data obtained from Meta about a person may not be used "for any purpose other than as reasonably necessary to support messaging with that person". Information from one customer's chat may not be forwarded "with any other customer". That rule directly limits any future feature that shares saved items between students.

**Storing user files on the team's own servers.** Nothing found forbids a business from storing what users send it on its own infrastructure. The [Meta Terms](https://www.facebook.com/legal/Meta-Terms-for-WhatsApp-Business-Platform) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary), Section 5.4, in fact expect it: "Meta does not provide an archiving service, storage service, or any backup functionality", and the business is "solely responsible for creating backups". Section 4.1 limits sharing Platform Data with third parties other than a Solution Provider, and forbids using it "to track, build, or augment profiles on individual WhatsApp Users". Storing a student's own files in the team's private Azure account, to serve that same student, appears to fit within these terms. That is analysis; Azure acts as the team's processor, not as a recipient Meta would treat as a "third party".

### 3. Encryption and data handling on the Cloud API

**Encryption and decryption.** According to Meta's [Data Privacy & Security page for the Cloud API](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/data-privacy-and-security) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Primary), a message "travels encrypted via WhatsApp between the user and Cloud API", then "Cloud API decrypts the message and forwards it to the business". "Cloud API manages the encryption/decryption keys on behalf of the business", and "Cloud API messages at rest are encrypted". Meta "acts as a data processor/service provider on behalf of the business" for Cloud API.

**Retention.** "Messages have a maximum retention period of 30 days". User identifiers are deleted within 30 days of a message's last status update "unless otherwise directed". Third-party documentation from [360dialog](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/resources/architecture-and-security) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Low) repeats the 30-day figure. The search-engine snippet of the same Meta page also contained the sentence "WhatsApp cannot access any message content exchanged between users and businesses". The fetched version did not show it, so that wording may have been removed or changed.

**Media.** According to Meta's [Media reference](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/business-phone-numbers/media) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Primary), "Media IDs in webhooks expire after 7 days", which was cut from 30 days on 9 October 2025. Media download URLs "expire after 5 minutes". Media the business uploads "persist for 30 days". The size limits are:

- documents (PDF, DOCX, etc.): 100 MB
- images (JPEG/PNG): 5 MB
- audio (AAC/AMR/MP3/M4A/OGG-Opus): 16 MB

For Campus Recall, this means the bot must download each incoming file to Azure within 7 days, and in practice right away. Files above these limits never reach the bot.

**Local Storage.** Meta's [Local Storage page](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/local-storage.md) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary) describes an opt-in mode. Message bodies and media are kept at rest in a chosen country, with "data-in-use" held in Meta data centres worldwide for up to 60 minutes. An example on the page uses `"data_localization_region": "IN"` for India, but the full country list was not confirmed.

**Ads and AI training.** Meta's Cloud API page says "Cloud API will not automatically use WhatsApp messages to inform the ads that a person sees", and that "different terms could apply" if the business turns on Meta's AI features. No primary statement was found on whether Meta itself trains AI on Cloud API message content. The binding rule found is the other way round: Section 4.7 of the Meta Terms bars the business from letting Platform Data train AI models, and Section 7.7 limits Meta's own controller use to "safety, security and integrity". Low-tier sources ([qualimero.com](https://qualimero.com/en/blog/whatsapp-chat-privacy), accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: Low, tier: Low) say that chats linked to Meta's own "Business Agent" product are collected by Meta. Campus Recall would not use that product.

**How WhatsApp labels the chat.** According to the [WhatsApp Help Center, "About end-to-end encryption"](https://faq.whatsapp.com/general/28030015/?locale=en_US) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Primary), chats with businesses using the WhatsApp Business app are treated as end-to-end encrypted by default. However, "some can choose WhatsApp's parent company, Meta, to securely store messages and respond to customers". Chats using those optional services are not considered end-to-end encrypted, "we display this clearly in the chat", and a chat's encryption status "can't change without being visible to the user". If Meta AI is used, the chat shows "uses AI from Meta" under the business name. Meta's support documentation (search snippet only) adds that a different system message appears when a business manages messages with another company.

The exact current label wording for a Meta-hosted Cloud API chat was not found. Third-party summaries describe a tappable system message saying the business uses a secure service from Meta to manage the chat. The [WhatsApp Privacy Policy](https://www.whatsapp.com/legal/privacy-policy) (effective 4 Jan 2021 as served; accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary) says businesses "might be working with third-party service providers (which may include Meta)", and tells users to read the business's own privacy policy.

**What this means for the local-first promise.** This is analysis. A Campus Recall WhatsApp bot breaks the app's "no query text leaves the device" promise for anyone who uses the bot:

- Query text and every file pass, in decrypted form, through Meta's Cloud API (kept for up to 30 days), then reach the team's Azure backend, where search would have to run.
- WhatsApp will label the chat as not end-to-end encrypted.

The bot therefore needs its own separate privacy notice that says this in plain terms. The PWA's existing promise should stay unchanged, and the bot should be described as an opt-in, server-side channel.

### 4. India: DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025

**What is in force when.** According to the [DPDP Rules 2025 as published in the Gazette](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Primary), Rule 1 sets three stages:

- Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21, which cover the Data Protection Board, started on publication.
- Rule 4, which covers Consent Managers, starts one year after publication.
- Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 start "eighteen months after the date of publication". These cover notice, security, breach, retention, children, rights, cross-border transfer and the research exemption.

The [PIB explainer](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf) (17 Nov 2025; accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Primary) confirms notification on 14 November 2025 and the 18-month phasing. [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: High, tier: Established) gives the Act's own commencement stages:

- 13 November 2025: the Board, definitions and procedural provisions.
- 13 November 2026: Consent Manager provisions.
- 13 May 2027: the substantive duties (notice and consent, Data Fiduciary obligations, children's data, Data Principal rights).

So on 10 November 2026 the core DPDP duties are not yet in force. They become binding on about 13 May 2027, which is likely within the life of a long-running project. Commentators date the 18-month mark as 12, 13 or 14 May 2027.

**Proposal to shorten the timeline.** [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/meity-may-cut-compliance-timeline-for-key-dpdp-rules-to-12-months-126012201293_1.html) (Jan 2026; accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: Medium, tier: Established) reported that MeitY might cut the timeline to 12 months for Significant Data Fiduciaries. [dpdprules.org](https://dpdprules.org/blog/dpdp-18-months-to-12-months-proposal) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: Low, tier: Low), checked on 28 October 2026, found no amendment notified. That site also mentions a "Removal of Difficulties Order" (S.O. 5458(E)) that changes wording in Sections 9(1) and 10(2)(c)(ii) but no commencement dates. That order could not be verified from a primary source.

**Obligations once in force.** According to the Gazette Rules:

- **Notice (Rule 3).** The notice must "be presented and be understandable independently". It must give an "itemised description of such personal data", the specified purpose, and a link and means to withdraw consent "with the ease of doing so being comparable to that with which such consent was given", to exercise rights, and to complain to the Board.
- **Security (Rule 6).** Reasonable security safeguards include encryption, access control, logging, backups, and keeping logs and personal data "for a period of one year" for incident investigation. They also require security clauses in contracts with Data Processors, which here means Azure and arguably Meta.
- **Breach notice to students (Rule 7(1)).** Each affected Data Principal must be told about a breach "without delay".
- **Breach notice to the Board (Rule 7(2)).** The Board must be told without delay, with a detailed report "within seventy-two hours".
- **Rights and grievances (Rule 14).** The means to exercise rights must be published, and grievances answered within a period "not exceeding ninety days".
- **Children (Rule 10).** Verifiable parental consent is needed for a "child", meaning anyone under 18. Some first-year students may be 17.
- **Research exemption (Rule 16).** The Act does not apply to processing "necessary for research, archiving or statistical purposes" that meets the Second Schedule standards. A live service for students is unlikely to qualify (analysis).

**Penalties.** According to the PIB explainer, penalties reach up to ₹250 crore for failing to keep reasonable security safeguards, up to ₹200 crore for breach-notification and children's-data violations, and up to ₹50 crore for other violations.

**Does this apply to a student team?** This is analysis and not legal advice. The Act covers any "person" who decides the purpose and means of processing digital personal data, and its definition of person includes individuals and associations. A team that runs a bot collecting other students' phone numbers and files is very likely a Data Fiduciary. The personal/domestic-purpose exclusion would not cover a service offered to other people. If IIT Gandhinagar formally hosts or sponsors the bot, the Institute could be the fiduciary instead.

Until May 2027, the old IT Act Section 43A and the SPDI Rules 2011 continue. [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=IN&t=law) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: Medium, tier: Established) says the IT Act and the older Privacy Rules "still apply" until then. Those apply to a "body corporate", which an informal student team probably is not.

The safest course is to build to the DPDP Rules now. That means:

- a plain-language notice sent as the bot's first reply, plus a hosted privacy policy, which also meets Meta's privacy-policy requirement;
- explicit "I agree" consent before storing anything;
- "DELETE ALL" / "STOP" commands that erase Azure blobs and indexes;
- a contact email for grievances;
- a written breach plan with a 72-hour timeline;
- an age confirmation of 18 or over.

**Cross-border transfer.** Rule 15 allows transfer abroad subject to future government restrictions. Meta's Cloud API stores data in Meta data centres, which may be outside India, unless Local Storage is turned on. The Azure region should be chosen deliberately, for example Central India.

### Extra findings that matter

**Service-message pricing changed on 1 October 2026.** Meta's AI Providers page notes that Meta "resumed per-message charging for service messages" for all businesses on 1 October 2026. [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/tech/how-much-does-india-have-to-pay-for-whatsapp-messages-from-october-1) (accessed 2026-11-10, confidence: Medium, tier: Established) reports about ₹0.115 per service message in India, after 1,000 free service messages per business number each month. A bot replying to student queries is no longer free above that allowance. This belongs in the cost axis but affects whether the project can operate at all.

**Training on stored files is ruled out.** Any future plan to fine-tune the embedding or OCR models on files received over WhatsApp is blocked by Section 4.7, except for a model "for your exclusive use". Training on files uploaded through the PWA is not affected by this clause.

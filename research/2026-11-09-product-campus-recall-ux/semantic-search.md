## Search by meaning: results, weak matches and recent searches

**Explaining why a result matched.** Users do not routinely want explanations of search results. A CHI '24 interview study of 12 non-technical users found that people mostly ask "why this result?" when results look irrelevant or when the task is complex or high-stakes. They called Google's "why this result" panels too obvious, and they valued previews that let them jump to the relevant section of a page and controls that let them contest a result [Juneja et al., CHI '24 / arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.16509v2) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary; small US-only sample). For text where the query words may not appear, the established engineering pattern is passage highlighting rather than term highlighting. According to [Elastic docs](https://www.elastic.co/guide/en/elasticsearch/reference/8.19/highlighting.html) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary), the semantic highlighter returns the fragments of a field that are closest in meaning to the query, ordered by score. It does not mark keywords. Comparable personal-memory products point the user to a location rather than a phrase. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/ios-use-natural-language-search-photos/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established) reports that iOS 18.1 Photos can find a moment inside a video, such as "baby crawling", and take the user straight to it. Microsoft's [Recall support page](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/windows/retrace-your-steps-with-recall-aa03f8a0-a78b-4b3e-b0a1-2eb8ac48701c) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary) splits results into "text matches" and "visual matches". It defines close matches as typically containing a search term and related matches as sharing "a commonality" with the query, and it opens the exact snapshot. A Recall hands-on found the text/visual split poorly differentiated in practice [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/2535272/microsoft-recall-windows-ai-tested-hands-on.html) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). Word highlighting on screenshots is harder than it looks. In the open-source Rewind alternative screenpipe, a maintainer removed a yellow keyword-highlight feature as "not great" [screenpipe GitHub thread](https://github-users.yhajoa.workers.dev/mediar-ai/screenpipe/pull/1330) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**). This suggests Campus Recall's approach of showing the best passage plus a page or time chip matches what comparable products do. A short "close / related" style label may help more than per-word highlighting, which would often have nothing to mark.

**Weak and zero results; scores.** For e-commerce, Baymard finds that nearly 50% of sites give users no effective way to recover from a search with no results. Generic search tips "are not sufficient on their own" because users rarely read or apply them. Its recommended recovery paths include suggested alternative queries and related categories [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/no-results-page) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; updated 2025-02-18). An older Baymard figure says 68% of sites have a no-results page that is a dead end. That number comes from a different benchmark year, so the two figures differ by date rather than by method. Evidence on showing similarity scores is mixed. Shani and Tractinsky (SIGIR 2013) found that users understood graphical relevance displays and that the scores led them to explore further down the list. Numeric scores had less effect than bars [BGU CRIS](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/displaying-relevance-scores-for-search-results/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary; pre-2021). A follow-up study reportedly found that confidence displays did not make relevant items easier to identify, although users liked them [BGU CRIS, confidence displays](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/investigating-confidence-displays-for-top-n-recommendations/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: Primary; seen in a search-result summary only). Obsidian Smart Connections shows a similarity score as an underline but says the value and range change with the embedding model [Smart Connections README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/brianpetro/obsidian-smart-connections@main/README.md) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**). A related plugin, Smart Linker, turns its score display off by default [Obsidian community](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/smart-linker) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**). The pattern indicates that raw cosine numbers are model-dependent and uncalibrated, so a percentage would overstate precision. Coarse bands such as "strong / related" are easier to defend. The current honest no-match copy ("does not mean you never saved it") fits Baymard's finding, but on its own it still ends the search with no way forward.

**Submit vs. as-you-type, and loading states.** Nielsen's limits are still the standard reference. Users perceive 0.1 s as instant, a wait past about 1 s needs a busy indicator to keep their flow, and past 10 s users need a percent-done indicator and a way to cancel [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Established; also echoed in NN/g's [skeleton-screens](https://nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/) guidance, which calls indicators unnecessary under 1 s). Algolia's docs say search-as-you-type lags on slow connections, recommend raising the debounce delay or disabling instant search there, and show a loading indicator when a search stalls [Algolia docs](https://www.algolia.com/doc/guides/building-search-ui/going-further/improve-performance/react) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Developer guides add that debouncing does not prevent out-of-order responses, so stale results need cancelling [DEV Community](https://dev.to/ahr_dev/beyond-debounce-the-complete-guide-to-fast-smooth-search-ux-442d) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**). Latency matters even at Google's scale. Google paused the Ask Photos rollout in June 2025 because it "isn't where it needs to be, in terms of latency, quality and ux", promising to "bring back the speed and recall" of classic search [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/google-has-paused-the-ask-photos-rollout-182409437.html) and [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-photos-ask-photos-pause-3563859) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Established). For queries that take a few hundred milliseconds, this suggests submit-on-Enter is defensible. A results area that appears within about 1 s needs no spinner, a short inline indicator covers the 1–10 s range, and only the first model load needs real progress.

**Recent searches and suggestions.** In NN/g's e-commerce study, users picked a suggested query in 23% of cases (31 of 136, ±7%). Even suggestions they did not pick showed them what the site contains, and suggestions that lead to zero results frustrate users [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/site-search-suggestions/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; 2018). Baymard recommends no more than 10 suggestions on desktop and 4–8 on mobile [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/autocomplete-design) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). Neither source covers recent searches or their privacy. Platform practice is consistent. Apple Photos keeps "the last few" searches under "Recently Searched" on the Search tab with a Clear button [iPhoneLife](https://iphonelife.com/content/how-to-find-recent-searches-photos-app-iphone) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**). Spotlight on macOS Tahoe keeps a history the user can delete [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/do-more-with-spotlight-in-macos-tahoe/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). Google Photos lets users remove individual recent queries, but no clear-all control is confirmed [allthings.how](https://allthings.how/how-to-clear-search-history-in-google-photos/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**). Recall's support page describes no search history at all, and its homepage instead surfaces recent activity. Recall also filters sensitive content and private browsing by default (Microsoft support, above). The pattern indicates that a short list kept on the device, shown on the empty search screen and easy to clear, is the norm. On a shared or borrowed phone, an opt-out matters because queries reveal what a student has saved.

> Conflicts noted: Baymard reports 68% (older benchmark) vs. "nearly 50%" (2025 update) of sites with dead-end or unhelpful no-results pages; these come from different benchmark years. Reviews of Microsoft Recall disagree on quality (PCWorld calls the text/visual split undifferentiated; Microsoft's docs present it as meaningful). Score-display research disagrees on value: scores change user behaviour (Shani & Tractinsky) but may not improve accuracy (follow-up study, seen only in a summary).
> Gaps: No primary sources found for how Mem, Notion AI Q&A, Readwise or Raycast phrase weak or zero results. No 2021–2026 controlled study found on term vs. passage highlighting for semantic search, or on recent-search privacy expectations. Apple's own documentation of video-moment results (timestamp labelling) not found. Raycast ranking/frecency: no sources found.

**Implications for Campus Recall (synthesis).** Keep the best-passage excerpt and the page or time chip. They match what Apple Photos and Recall do, and term highlighting would often have nothing to mark. Replace a single "no strong matches" dead end with one or two recovery actions, such as showing related matches below the threshold behind a labelled toggle or suggesting a shorter query, and avoid showing a raw similarity percentage. Submit-on-Enter fits the latency profile if results show within about 1 s with a light inline indicator, stale queries are cancelled, and a real progress bar is kept for the first model load only. If recent searches are added, keep about 5 on the device only, show them on the empty search screen with per-item remove and Clear all, and let users turn them off.

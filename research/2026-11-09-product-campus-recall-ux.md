# Research Report — Campus Recall UX

> **Type:** product (custom axes) | **Date:** 2026-11-09 | **Constraints:** phones first (Android and iPhone), Indian students, local-first, no remote inference, explicit 445 MB model setup (spec S7), immediate permanent delete (spec S3)
>
> **Goals:**
> - How should an app ask for a large one-time on-device model download, and what can it offer before the download finishes?
> - How should search by meaning show why a result matched, handle weak or no matches, and support recent searches?
> - How should a single-page PWA handle focus, titles, Back and errors when the screen changes?
> - What do Android, iPhone and the Indian phone context mean for install, sharing into the app, and keeping data?
> - How do comparable local-first and personal-library apps handle first run, empty states, optional sync and deleting?
>
> **Assumptions:** the reader is the three-person student team building the pilot. "Comparable apps" means personal-library and local-first tools. Recommendations must fit the frozen spec statements S1–S15.

## Where Campus Recall stands

The team's audit (Nielsen's heuristics, Krug's laws, a walkthrough of the first five minutes and a WCAG 2.2 AA spot check) scores the deployed pilot 5 out of 10, and about 6 with the undeployed `codex/mobile-polish` branch, which already fixes scroll position on screen change, the iOS zoom on the status filter, safe-area padding and landscape navigation. Twelve findings remain. Three are major and break flow in the first minutes: the first search is a dead end leading to a jargon-heavy 445 MB download in Settings (F1); status is missing or raw and several actions give no feedback (F2); and errors are raw, global and sticky (F3). Eight are minor: focus and title on screen change (F4), cluttered empty screens (F5), Back and Escape (F6), adding files (F7), the no-match dead end (F8), Auto-sync labels (F9), manifest colour and menu (F10), and install and storage (F11). F12 is cosmetic jargon. Each section below sets out what the guidelines say, where they disagree, and how Campus Recall compares.

## First run with a large one-time download

**Where and when to ask.** On-device AI guidance says to ask at the moment of use. web.dev says to "only download what's necessary", wait for "reasonable certainty the AI features will be used" (its example starts a type-ahead model when the user begins typing), and warn before large downloads [web.dev, Client-side AI performance](https://web.dev/articles/client-side-ai-performance) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Chrome enforces this: while a model is `downloadable` or `downloading`, `create()` needs a user activation [Chrome for Developers, built-in AI](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/get-started), and Translator packs are "downloaded on demand" on first use [Chrome for Developers, Translator API](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/translator-api) (both accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). The Transformers.js Whisper Web demo loads its model on first use, labelled "Loading model files... (only run once)" with one bar per file [xenova/whisper-web](https://github.com/xenova/whisper-web) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Low; demo repository, last commit 2023). NN/g reports that requests tied to the user's own action surprise less, and that a stated reason raised grants by 12% [NN/g, Permission Requests](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/permission-requests/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established); a USENIX Security 2021 study found asking in context "always has a positive effect" [Elbitar et al., USENIX Security 2021](https://trust.cispa.saarland/publication/elbitar-usenix-21/elbitar-usenix-21.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary; pre-2022). "Ask in context" is High confidence, though carrying permission research over to downloads is inference.

**Size, network, time and control.** Chrome calls it "best practice to alert the user to the time required", with a progress bar from `downloadprogress` events, an indeterminate state during extraction, and only the remaining bytes after an interruption [Chrome for Developers, Inform users of model download](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/inform-users-of-model-download) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary). web.dev adds "time remaining", notes mobile users tolerate large downloads less than desktop users, and says to "inform the user... and continue the download when the connection is restored" [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/client-side-ai-performance) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). NN/g wants percent-done for waits of 10 s or more [NN/g, Progress Indicators](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). Chrome's caching guide shows chunked fetches with progress and an `AbortSignal` to cancel, and recommends `navigator.storage.persist()` because unpersisted storage "may be cleared under storage pressure" [Chrome for Developers, Cache models in the browser](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/cache-models). Chrome fetches its own Gemini Nano only on an "unlimited data or an unmetered connection" [Chrome get-started](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/get-started). Google Translate offers "Ask before downloading" or "Use Wi-Fi or mobile network" [Google Translate Help](https://support.google.com/translate/answer/6142473?hl=en), and Google Maps updates offline areas only on Wi-Fi [Google Maps Help](https://support.google.com/gmm/answer/6291838?hl=en) (all four accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary).

A web app cannot reliably detect metered data. Per MDN's compat data, Safari and iOS never supported `navigator.connection`, Firefox removed it, and `type` (`wifi`/`cellular`) is experimental and effectively Chrome-for-Android-only [MDN browser-compat-data, NetworkInformation.json](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/api/NetworkInformation.json); [MDN, Network Information API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Network_Information_API) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary). `saveData` is usually false since Chrome 100 removed Lite mode, its only trigger according to a blink-dev participant [blink-dev, Save-Data thread](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/HR7tWmewbSA/m/9UrOsc9AAAAJ); [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/google-is-retiring-chromes-data-saving-lite-mode-next-month-saying-its-no-longer-necessary/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary/Established). Background Fetch is experimental and Chromium-only, whereas `storage.persist()` and `storage.estimate()` work in every major engine [MDN BCD](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary).

**What to offer before the download completes.** web.dev advises letting "key features function even if the AI model isn't fully loaded yet" [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/client-side-ai-performance), and Chrome cites Miravia serving a cloud model until the local one is ready [Chrome inform-users](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/inform-users-of-model-download) (both accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Campus Recall's on-device promise rules out cloud fallback, so a local title or keyword search is the nearest equivalent (inference). Platforms stage large assets: Play Asset Delivery has `install-time`, `fast-follow` and `on-demand` modes [Android Developers, Play Asset Delivery](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/asset-delivery), and Apple's Background Assets has Essential, Prefetch and On-demand packs [Apple WWDC25 session 325](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/325/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary). Google Translate lets users "upgrade" a language pack later [Google Translate Help](https://support.google.com/translate/answer/6142473?hl=en) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Apple Intelligence downloads up to 8 GB (14 GB on newer devices) after an OS update [Apple Support 121115](https://support.apple.com/en-us/121115) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Forum reports say Apple Photos shows partial results with a note that more will appear after indexing [Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256112241) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: Low).

**Drop-off evidence** is old and indirect. Google Play reported a 1% install-conversion drop per 6 MB for apps under 100 MB, but only 1% per 200 MB for 500–3,000 MB games [Google Play, Shrinking APKs (2017)](https://medium.com/googleplaydev/shrinking-apks-growing-installs-5d3fcba23ce2); [Android Developers, game size](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/game-size) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Poki's 2018 W3C paper found that at 10 MB about 80% of US players got to play, against under 60% in Egypt [W3C Games Workshop, Size Matters](https://www.w3.org/2018/12/games-workshop/papers/size-matters.html) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: Primary; single pre-2022 dataset). Drop-off grows with size and weaker networks, but nothing measures an opt-in 445 MB download after install.

> Conflicts noted: web.dev says connection type "won't actually indicate whether the user is on Wifi or data", while MDN BCD lists `type` on Chrome for Android; web.dev probably means `effectiveType`, and `type` is absent on iOS and desktop either way. A SitePen blog claims Firefox Android support; MDN shows it removed in version 99, and MDN is used. Apple's storage figure changed between OS versions (7 GB earlier, now 8 or 14 GB).
> Gaps: no 2022–2026 measurement of drop-off from an optional in-app model download; no Apple dictation-download UX; no first-party description of Translate's or Maps' size and time disclosure; no web pause/resume guidance beyond chunking and `AbortSignal`; WebLLM Chat's progress UI not inspected.

**How Campus Recall compares (synthesis).** A search before setup shows a red "Set up offline search in Settings first" error with no button; Settings then describes the download in jargon, starts all 445 MB at once with no Wi-Fi note, and shows raw file names as progress (F1, F12). The app rightly waits for a tap (S7), but the offer is in the wrong place and lacks size, time and network context. It should become a one-tap card where the search failed, with a reason, the size, a Wi-Fi suggestion and plain progress ("212 of 445 MB · about 3 min on Wi-Fi"), after which the typed search runs. Since metered data cannot be detected on iOS or desktop, the app should state the size and let the student choose. A small text pack first, with vision, speech and OCR later or on demand, and a clearly labelled partial title search until then, would mirror the staging patterns.

## Search by meaning: results, weak matches and recent searches

**Explaining why a result matched.** A CHI '24 interview study of 12 non-technical users found people ask "why this result?" mostly when results look irrelevant or stakes are high, and value previews that jump to the relevant section over explanation panels [Juneja et al., CHI '24 / arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.16509v2) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary; small US-only sample). When query words may be absent, the pattern is passage highlighting: Elastic's semantic highlighter returns the fragments closest in meaning without marking keywords [Elastic docs](https://www.elastic.co/guide/en/elasticsearch/reference/8.19/highlighting.html) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Personal-memory products point to a location: iOS 18.1 Photos jumps to the moment in a video [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/ios-use-natural-language-search-photos/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established), and Microsoft Recall splits "text matches" from "visual matches" sharing "a commonality" and opens the exact snapshot [Recall support page](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/windows/retrace-your-steps-with-recall-aa03f8a0-a78b-4b3e-b0a1-2eb8ac48701c) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary), though a hands-on found the split poorly differentiated [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/2535272/microsoft-recall-windows-ai-tested-hands-on.html) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). A screenpipe maintainer removed yellow keyword highlighting as "not great" [screenpipe GitHub thread](https://github-users.yhajoa.workers.dev/mediar-ai/screenpipe/pull/1330) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**).

**Weak and zero results; scores.** Baymard finds nearly 50% of e-commerce sites offer no effective recovery from zero results, that generic tips "are not sufficient on their own", and that alternative queries and related categories work better [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/no-results-page) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; updated 2025-02-18). Score displays are contested. Shani and Tractinsky (SIGIR 2013) found graphical relevance displays led users further down the list, with numbers weaker than bars [BGU CRIS](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/displaying-relevance-scores-for-search-results/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary; pre-2021); a follow-up reportedly found confidence displays did not help users identify relevant items [BGU CRIS, confidence displays](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/investigating-confidence-displays-for-top-n-recommendations/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: Primary; summary only). Obsidian Smart Connections warns its similarity value changes with the embedding model [Smart Connections README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/brianpetro/obsidian-smart-connections@main/README.md), and Smart Linker hides scores by default [Obsidian community](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/smart-linker) (both accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**). Raw cosine numbers are uncalibrated, so bands such as "strong / related" are easier to defend than a percentage.

**Submit vs. as-you-type, and loading.** Nielsen's limits hold: 0.1 s feels instant, past about 1 s a busy indicator keeps flow, past 10 s users need percent-done and cancel [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Established; echoed by NN/g's [skeleton-screens](https://nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/) guidance: no indicator under 1 s). Algolia says search-as-you-type lags on slow connections and recommends longer debounce or submit-only there [Algolia docs](https://www.algolia.com/doc/guides/building-search-ui/going-further/improve-performance/react) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary); stale out-of-order responses need cancelling [DEV Community](https://dev.to/ahr_dev/beyond-debounce-the-complete-guide-to-fast-smooth-search-ux-442d) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**). Google paused Ask Photos in June 2025 because it "isn't where it needs to be, in terms of latency, quality and ux" [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/google-has-paused-the-ask-photos-rollout-182409437.html); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-photos-ask-photos-pause-3563859) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Established). For queries of a few hundred milliseconds, submit-on-Enter is defensible.

**Recent searches.** In NN/g's study, users picked a suggestion 23% of the time, unpicked suggestions still revealed the site's contents, and suggestions leading to zero results frustrated users [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/site-search-suggestions/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established; 2018); Baymard advises 4–8 suggestions on mobile [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/autocomplete-design) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). Neither covers recent searches. Apple Photos keeps "the last few" with a Clear button [iPhoneLife](https://iphonelife.com/content/how-to-find-recent-searches-photos-app-iphone) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**); macOS Tahoe Spotlight keeps deletable history [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/do-more-with-spotlight-in-macos-tahoe/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established); Google Photos removes single queries, with no confirmed clear-all [allthings.how](https://allthings.how/how-to-clear-search-history-in-google-photos/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**). Recall keeps no search history and filters sensitive content by default (Microsoft support, above). The norm is a short, clearable on-device list; on a shared phone an opt-out matters, because queries reveal what a student saved.

> Conflicts noted: Baymard's older 68% dead-end figure and its 2025 "nearly 50%" differ by benchmark year. Reviewers disagree on whether Recall's text/visual split is meaningful (PCWorld vs Microsoft). Scores change behaviour (Shani & Tractinsky) but may not improve accuracy (follow-up, summary only).
> Gaps: no primary sources on how Mem, Notion AI Q&A, Readwise or Raycast phrase weak results; no 2021–2026 controlled study of term vs passage highlighting or of recent-search privacy; no Apple documentation of video-moment timestamps; nothing on Raycast frecency.

**How Campus Recall compares (synthesis).** The best-passage excerpt with a page or time chip already matches Apple Photos and Recall and should stay; term highlighting would often have nothing to mark. The gaps are F8: the honest no-match copy ("does not mean you never saved it") still ends the search, an empty query fails silently, and there are no recent searches. Add one or two recovery actions (related matches below the threshold behind a labelled toggle, or a shorter-query suggestion), avoid raw percentages, show results within about 1 s with a light inline indicator, and cancel stale queries. If recent searches are added, keep about five on the device with per-item remove, Clear all and an Off switch.

## Screen changes, Back, feedback and errors in a single-page PWA

**Route changes: focus and title.** A full page load moves focus and announces the title; a client-side route change does neither unless code does it [BBC GEL](https://bbc.github.io/gel/foundations/routing) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary). Sources agree focus must leave the tapped link but differ on the target. BBC GEL focuses the new `<h1>` (`tabindex="-1"`) except on first load and sets `document.title` to "Screen | Application"; [Angular](https://angular.dev/best-practices/a11y) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary) says focus "the main content header", never `body`; [Deque](https://www.deque.com/blog/accessible-routing-in-javascript-frameworks/) (Steadman, 2020; accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Established) ranks the heading first, then the main container, then a skip link with a live announcement. The only user study, [Gatsby with Fable Tech Labs](https://www.gatsbyjs.com/blog/2019-07-11-user-testing-accessible-client-routing/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established), had five participants: screen reader users preferred heading focus, magnification users gained little from live regions, and it settled on a skip link plus a live region. [Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/architecture/accessibility) announces the title, then the `<h1>`, then the path, and the Navigation API's `intercept()` defaults to focusing the first `autofocus` element or `<body>` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/NavigateEvent/intercept)) (both accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). The [DWP Accessibility Manual](https://accessibility-manual.dwp.gov.uk/guidance-for-your-job-role/software-engineer-or-frontend-developer) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary) wants titles of the form "H1 – service", prefixed "Error:" when errors show. All sources agree the title should change on every route.

**Back for overlays and viewers.** [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/back-button-expectations) (2020; accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Established) found users expect Back to close an overlay, mobile users rely on Back more, and 37% of tested sites got it wrong; it recommends `history.pushState()` on open. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/overuse-of-overlays/) (2015; accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Established) agrees. Forum reports say a viewer closed with its X must also call `history.back()`, and that installed Android PWAs can behave differently ([Ionic forum](https://forum.ionicframework.com/t/back-button-not-closing-modal-in-ionic-7-angular-17-capacitor-5/247947), accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**). Chromium's [history-manipulation intervention](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/127.0.6533.99/docs/history_manipulation_intervention.md) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary) skips entries added without a user activation, so the entry must come from the opening tap. `CloseWatcher` routes Esc and Android Back to a component ([Chrome 120 release notes](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-chrome-120)), grouping watchers created without activation so one Back closes them all ([MDN CloseWatcher](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/CloseWatcher)) (both accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). [web-features](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/closewatcher) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary) lists Chrome 126 and Firefox 149 (March 2026), confirmed by [Mozilla bug 1966073](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1966073) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary), but no Safari. The Navigation API became Baseline in early 2026 with Safari 26.2 and Firefox 147 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/baseline-navigation-api), [web-features](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/navigation), accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary).

**Toasts, inline messages and banners.** Per WCAG's [2.2.1 Understanding document](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/timing-adjustable.html) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary), an auto-dismissing toast is not a time limit only if its information is available elsewhere. [Roselli](https://adrianroselli.com/2020/01/defining-toast-messages.html) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: **Low**, recognised expert blog) and [GitHub Primer](https://primer.style/accessibility/toasts/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary) cite 2.2.1, 1.3.2, 2.1.1 and 4.1.3; Primer avoids toasts, using nothing when the result is visible, banners for complex successes and passive errors, and dialogs for interruptions. [Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/notification-pattern) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary) keeps inline and actionable notifications until dismissed and bars timed dismissal for critical messages; [Flutter](https://docs.flutter.dev/release/breaking-changes/snackbar-with-action-behavior-update) and [Angular Material](https://material.angular.io/components/snack-bar) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary) say snackbars with an action should not auto-dismiss. The [GOV.UK notification banner](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/notification-banner/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary) is one per page and removed "when the user moves to a new page", the closest source on clearing stale messages.

**Plain-language errors and feedback.** [GOV.UK's "problem with the service" pattern](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/problem-with-the-service-pages/) bans jargon and codes such as "500", says what happened to the user's data, and offers help; [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/) (2023) asks for plain language, specific causes, next steps, preserved input and no blame; [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts) rejects titles like "Error 329347 occurred" (all accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary/Established/Primary). [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/uxguide/mess-error) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary) allows a searchable code under "Show details", always with a text description. Copyable correlation IDs appear only in practitioner write-ups ([SSW Rules](https://www.ssw.com.au/rules-legacy/do-you-return-detailed-error-messages/), accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**), which note that auto-hiding errors leave no time to copy them. [NN/g on system status](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visibility-system-status/) (Harley, 2018; accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established) calls feedback on actions basic, since without it users repeat actions and lose trust; [Apple HIG Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary) confirms only significant actions, as people "only need to know when it doesn't" succeed.

> Conflicts noted: (1) Focus target after a route change: h1 (BBC, Angular, Deque), skip link plus live region (Gatsby), announcer only (Next.js), body/autofocus (Navigation API). (2) CloseWatcher in Chrome: 120 (release notes) vs 126 (web-features), probably a later behaviour change; 126 is used. (3) Confirm every action (NN/g) vs only significant ones (Apple, Primer); reconciled if every action gets visible feedback, not necessarily a toast.
> Gaps: no Material 3 snackbar timing text (page renders in JavaScript); no GOV.UK SPA focus guidance; no primary source on user-facing correlation IDs; nothing on a global `role=alert` banner persisting across routes beyond GOV.UK's rule; no field data on pushState in installed Android PWAs.

**How Campus Recall compares (synthesis).** Mobile polish now resets scroll, but focus stays on the tapped link and `index.html` sets one fixed title (F4). The viewer is not in history, so Android Back leaves the screen, and Escape does not close the cloud viewer (F6). One `role=alert` banner survives navigation and shows server and storage strings word for word (F3). Sign out, sync now, cloud delete, downloads and retry give no feedback, items sit at "Queued" with no reason, and engine failures not tied to an item never appear (F2). Each misses a High-confidence rule. On each hash change, set the title to "Screen – Campus Recall" and focus the `<h1>` (visible ring) except on first load. Push a history entry from the opening tap, close on `popstate`, call `history.back()` on X, and add `CloseWatcher` for Chrome and Firefox. Clear the banner on route change, show errors inline at their source as plain sentences with a short copyable reference (raw string in logs), and give every silent action visible feedback, using toasts only when their content is also visible elsewhere so the 6.5-second dismissal raises no 2.2.1 issue.

## Phone platform and Indian context

**Storage durability.** Safari's 7-day cap (2020) deletes script-writable storage after seven days of Safari use without interaction on the site, but WebKit says Home Screen web apps "have their own counter of days of use" and that data loss there would be "a serious bug" [WebKit blog, 2020-03-24](https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary). web.dev says the policy "does not apply to installed PWAs that have been added to the home screen" [web.dev Storage for the web](https://web.dev/articles/storage-for-the-web) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary); MDN still describes the rule without addressing Home Screen apps [MDN Storage quotas](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary). Since iOS 17, origins may use up to 60% of disk with no prompt, Home Screen apps get "the same origin quota and overall quota", eviction is per-origin LRU excluding persistent origins, and `persist()` is granted by heuristics "like whether the website is opened as a Home Screen Web App" [WebKit Updates to Storage Policy, 2023-08-10](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary). A 2018 WebKit bug documents that Home Screen apps keep storage separate from Safari [WebKit Bug 181849](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=181849) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: Primary but dated), consistent with web.dev's "separate storage container"; models fetched in a Safari tab would then be fetched again after install. Chromium never prompts for `persist()`, granting it silently on engagement, installation and notification permission [web.dev Persistent storage](https://web.dev/articles/persistent-storage) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary); the original intent named "added to home screen" as a signal [blink-dev Intent to Experiment: Durable Storage](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/d/msg/blink-dev/5Sihi1iAXYc/wnvNDFIPAQAJ) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary, 2016). Firefox shows a popup, and Chrome evicts whole least-recently-used non-persistent origins (MDN; web.dev).

**Install promotion and sharing.** `beforeinstallprompt` works in Chrome, Edge, Opera and Samsung Internet but not Firefox or Safari [MDN beforeinstallprompt](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/beforeinstallprompt_event) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary). Chrome requires a tap, 30 seconds on the page, HTTPS and a complete manifest [web.dev install criteria](https://web.dev/articles/install-criteria), having dropped the `fetch`-handler requirement in Chrome 108/112 [Chrome blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/update-install-criteria); web.dev advises custom install UI only after "strong interest signals", ideally right after a completed task [web.dev Patterns for promoting install](https://web.dev/articles/promote-install) (all three accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). On iOS 26, "every website added to the Home Screen opens as a web app" by default [WebKit WWDC25](https://webkit.org/blog/16993/news-from-wwdc25-web-technology-coming-this-fall-in-safari-26-beta/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary), as [iDownloadBlog](https://www.idownloadblog.com/?p=1042117) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established) confirms, but install is still manual via Share. Web Share Target works in Chromium browsers (Chrome for Android 76+) but not Firefox or Safari [MDN BCD share_target](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/manifests/webapp/share_target.json) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary), as a December 2025 developer note confirms for iOS [plyr.fm research note](https://tangled.org/zzstoatzz.io/plyr.fm/blob/main/docs/research/2025-12-19-web-share-target-ios-pwa.md) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**). It needs an installed app, a POST multipart `share_target` with an `accept` list, and a service worker reading `formData()`, so it can work offline [Chrome Developers Web Share Target](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/web-apis/web-share-target) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary).

**theme_color.** On Android, `theme_color` colours the status bar and `background_color` the splash screen [web.dev Learn PWA: App design](https://web.dev/learn/pwa/app-design) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary); a per-page `<meta name="theme-color">` overrides the manifest and accepts `prefers-color-scheme` media queries [MDN theme_color](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/Manifest/Reference/theme_color) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary). In Safari 26, `theme-color` reportedly applies only to installed web apps [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/801239); [MDN BCD PR 28503](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/pull/28503) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: Low), and a Glide community user reports the `black-translucent` status bar broke in iOS 26.1 (confidence: Low, tier: **Low**; no URL recorded in the notes).

**Indian phone context.** India's mobile web traffic in September 2026 was 93.89% Android and 6.05% iOS [StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/india) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Established). Apple held 9% (Counterpoint) or 10% (IDC) of 2025 shipments [GSMArena on IDC](https://www.gsmarena.com/indian_smartphone_market_remains_flat_in_2025_signals_structural_changes-news-71575.php); [Business Today on Counterpoint](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/over-1-in-5-smartphones-sold-in-india-cost-more-than-rs-30000-apple-sees-highest-ever-value-share-report-514235-2026-02-02) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Established), and 2026 memory prices pushed budget phones back to 4 GB RAM and 64–128 GB storage [Counterpoint launch tracker](https://counterpointresearch.com/cn/reports/India-Smartphone-Launches-Q1-2026-OEMs-Re-calibrate-Portfolios-Amid-Rising-BOM-Costs) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). TRAI puts wireless data at ₹8.02 per GB in FY2025-26 [TRAI PR 127/2026](https://trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-10/PR_No127of2026.pdf); [TelecomTalk](https://telecomtalk.info/revenue-per-gb-data-drops-fy26-trai/1012551/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary/Established), so 445 MB costs about ₹3.5. The binding limit is the 1.5–2 GB daily cap on common prepaid plans, such as Jio's ₹299 plan [The Week, 2024-07-01](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2024/07/01/jio-airtel-vodafone-idea-tariff-hike-detailed-comparison-of-old-revised-price.amp.html) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). Android's system Data Saver remains though Chrome's Lite mode is gone [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/google-is-retiring-chromes-data-saving-lite-mode-next-month-saying-its-no-longer-necessary/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established). IIT Gandhinagar is described as Wi-Fi throughout, with reported speeds from 4 Mbps to several hundred Mbps [Careers360](https://www.careers360.com/university/indian-institute-of-technology-gandhinagar/facilities); [Shiksha reviews](https://www.shiksha.com/college/iit-gandhinagar-indian-institute-of-technology-32705/reviews-2) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: Low).

> Conflicts noted: Safari's quota is about 1 GB with prompts per web.dev (2024) but 60% of disk with no prompt per WebKit (2023) and MDN (2026); web.dev appears pre-iOS 17, so WebKit is used. On the 7-day cap, web.dev says Home Screen apps are exempt and WebKit says they keep their own use-based counter, which amounts to the same for an app the student opens. Firt.dev once reported service-worker storage shared with Safari on iOS, contradicting WebKit bug 181849. Apple's 2025 India share is 9% (Counterpoint) or 10% (IDC).
> Gaps: no official Apple documentation of iOS 26 `theme_color` or splash behaviour; no India shipment split by RAM or storage; no official IIT Gandhinagar network specification; no data on Data Saver use among Indian students; Chromium's current `persist()` heuristics not verified beyond documentation.

**How Campus Recall compares (synthesis).** Persistence is requested only from a manual button that stays visible after it is granted, there is no install offer, and nothing protects iPhone Safari users from the 7-day cap (F11); `theme_color` is #245edb blue while the app is paper #f4f1ea (F10); and there is no share target (F7). With about 94% of Indian traffic on Android Chromium, where `persist()` is silent and installation is a strong signal, the app should call `persist()` automatically after install and after the first save and report the result in plain words. iPhone students should be steered to Add to Home Screen *before* the 445 MB download, since Home Screen storage is separate and protected. The download costs about ₹3.5 but takes a large bite of a 1.5 GB daily cap and a 64–128 GB phone, so "use campus Wi-Fi" and the size belong upfront. A share target ("save from WhatsApp/Gallery") reaches most Android users and no iPhone users, so it is an Android enhancement beside the universal picker. `theme_color` should become the paper colour, with a dark-scheme variant.

## Comparable local-first apps: first run, optional sync, deleting

*Method note: app observations come from vendor help pages or third-party write-ups read on 2026-11-09; no app was installed. Treat each as dated 2026-11-09.*

**First run and empty libraries.** Local-first tools open with a choice of where data lives, not a pitch: Obsidian's first launch asks only whether to create a vault or open a folder [Obsidian Help](https://obsidian.md/help/Getting+started/Create+a+vault) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Logseq asks to open or create a graph, yet a 2022 thread reports a new user spending over 4.5 hours in the demo graph without knowing where files went ([Logseq forum](https://discuss.logseq.com/t/first-launch-confusion-for-new-users-no-information-on-what-a-graph-is-how-files-are-read-from-written-to/4297), accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**). Cloud libraries seed starter content: Readwise Reader adds a "Getting Started with Reader" document ([Readwise Help](https://help.readwise.io/article/163-how-do-i-get-started-with-reader), accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary), and Notion opens a learn-by-doing page and checklist ([Appcues GoodUX](https://goodux.appcues.com/blog/notions-lightweight-onboarding) and a Packt book, accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established/Low). mymind avoids dropdowns and filters altogether ([mymind manifesto](https://mymind.com/manifesto), accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: Primary). [NN/g, Kaplan 2021](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established) asks empty states to communicate status, give learning cues and offer direct pathways to key tasks, and warns that an empty filtered view leaves users wondering whether the filters are wrong.

**Onboarding research.** In [NN/g's tutorial study](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tutorials/) (Kendrick, 2020; accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established) of 70 participants, tutorial users reached 91% task success against 94% for skippers (not significant) and rated tasks harder (SEQ 4.92 vs 5.49, p=0.047). A companion [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-app-onboarding/) article (2020; accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Established) advises skipping onboarding and promotional tours unless the app needs user information or has unfamiliar workflows. Vendor benchmarks disagree: Userpilot reports 19.2% average checklist completion while Appcues sets a 60–70% target ([Appcues](https://appcues.com/blog/onboarding-optimization), accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**/vendor), and Pendo's time-to-value best-in-class of 0.2 days could not be verified ([Pendo](https://www.pendo.io/product-benchmarks/), accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: vendor).

**Optional sync.** Sync is usually a separate opt-in, often with an explicit encryption decision first. Obsidian Sync's setup runs 27 sub-steps, says "Do not start syncing yet", uploads only when the user presses Start Syncing, and then shows status as one status-bar icon with a green check when "Fully Synced" [Obsidian Help](https://obsidian.md/help/sync/setup) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Logseq Sync uses a separate graph password it does not store ([Logseq docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/logseq/docs/master/pages/Logseq%20Sync.md)); Anytype sets local-only mode from the onboarding screen and uses a 12-word key it cannot reset ([Anytype docs](https://doc.anytype.io/anytype-docs/advanced/data-and-security/self-hosting/local-only)); Bear's iCloud sync is one Pro toggle ([Bear FAQ](https://bear.app/faq/features-and-price-of-bear-pro/)); Day One encrypts new journals end to end irreversibly ([Day One guide](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/settings/how-to-set-up-end-to-end-encryption/)); Standard Notes works "totally offline without an account" ([Standard Notes Help](https://StandardNotes.org/help/59/can-i-use-standard-notes-totally-offline)); and Joplin shows a Synchronisation Status page with item counts ([Joplin docs](https://joplinapp.org/help/apps/sync/nextcloud)) (all six accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). [Ink & Switch, Kleppmann et al., 2019](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/local-first/) (accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary, peer-reviewed at Onward! 2019) holds that "the network is optional", asks apps to "make clear to users when the data is stored only on their device versus being transmitted to a backend", and criticises alarming "offline!" warnings. A summary of Eileen Wagner's Local-First Conf talk argues for either explicit or fully non-intrusive sync, never a mix ([second-brain summary](https://second-brain-nuxt.vercel.app/the-ux-of-local-first), accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Low, tier: **Low**).

**Deleting.** Most of these apps soft-delete: Apple Notes for 30 days ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/guide/icloud/mm2f42f05cb9)), Google Keep for 7 days ([Google Keep Help](https://support.google.com/keep/answer/6262770?hl=en-GB)), Raindrop until Trash is emptied ([Raindrop Help](https://help.raindrop.io/troubleshooting/lost-data)), and Obsidian offers trash or permanent deletion ([Obsidian Help](https://obsidian.md/help/settings)) (all four accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Apple's HIG avoids alerts for common, undoable actions "even when they're destructive" and keeps them for uncommon, irreversible ones ([Apple HIG Alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts), accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary); Material says confirmation "isn't necessary when the consequences of an action are reversible" ([Material](https://m2.material.io/design/communication/confirmation-acknowledgement.html), accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Primary); and [NN/g, Nielsen](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/) (2018, reviewed 2026; accessed 2026-11-09, confidence: High, tier: Established) warns that overused confirmations get clicked through, wants specific labels such as "Delete file", and urges going "to great lengths to provide undo". Confirm-only-when-irreversible is therefore High confidence.

> Conflicts noted: onboarding benchmarks (Userpilot ~19% vs Appcues 60–70%) use different definitions and samples, so neither is reliable. The Wagner summary dates the talk 2026-02-22, but the Local-First Conf schedule lists it in 2025; treated as 2025. Standard Notes' help says no account is needed, but an App Store developer reply says one now is; unresolved.
> Gaps: no sources on the current first-run screens of Google Photos, OneNote, Day One or mymind, or on Anytype's bin; no peer-reviewed time-to-value study; the Material 3 snackbar page did not render, so the 4–10 s undo duration comes only from a third-party mirror (Low).

**How Campus Recall compares (synthesis, not sourced fact).** Comparable apps open with one decision or one starter document. Campus Recall shows a welcome modal repeating the landing page, then an empty Library with three competing calls to action and zero-count filter chips (F5). That argues for dropping the modal and giving the empty library one primary action, with chips hidden until items exist. Obsidian pauses before the first upload, which supports keeping "Include this device's library" as explicit consent while merging sign-in and consent into one screen and making "Use Local only" a positive choice rather than a disguised Cancel (F9). Since S3 makes local delete permanent, a specific dialog ("Delete 'notes.pdf' from this device? This can't be undone.") is defensible, but cloud delete should end with visible success, and the team should weigh whether a short undo window would serve students better.

## Key Findings

**The model download should be offered where the student needs it, with its size and a Wi-Fi suggestion, and never started without a tap.** Chrome requires a user activation before a model download and calls showing progress and time best practice; web.dev says to download only when use is likely and to warn first; NN/g and a USENIX study found requests tied to the user's own action are accepted more often (High for asking in context). This fits S7's explicit setup and means the red "Set up offline search in Settings first" error should become an inline download card on Search. Mobile data cannot be detected reliably (`navigator.connection` is absent on iOS, and Lite mode, which sent Save-Data, ended in 2022), so the app should state the size and let the student choose.

**On iPhone, installing to the Home Screen comes before trusting the library; on Android, persistence can be requested quietly.** WebKit and web.dev agree that Safari tabs lose script-written storage after 7 days without use while Home Screen apps keep theirs (High). Chrome grants `persist()` silently, weighting engagement and installation (High). Android carries about 94% of Indian mobile traffic (StatCounter, September 2026), and Web Share Target works only there (High). So: request persistence automatically after the first save, show an iPhone "Add to Home Screen" tip before the 445 MB download, and add Android sharing as a bonus, not the main way to add files.

**Every screen change needs a focus move and a new tab title, and Back must close the viewer.** Sources agree a single-page app must move focus and update `document.title` on each route (High), though they differ on the target; BBC GEL, Angular and Deque favour the `<h1>`. Baymard and NN/g agree users expect Back to close an overlay and that `history.pushState` is the fix (High); because Chromium skips entries not created by a tap, the entry must come from the tap that opens the viewer.

**Errors should be plain sentences, should leave when the student leaves the page, and should never be the only record of something important.** GOV.UK, NN/g and Apple reject jargon and bare codes (High). WCAG 2.2.1 allows an auto-dismissing toast only when its information is available elsewhere, and GOV.UK removes banners on a new page (Medium, one source). This points to an error-code-to-sentence map with raw detail behind "Show details", and to clearing the global banner on navigation.

**Search by meaning should show the matching passage and the exact place, avoid fake precision, and always offer a next step.** Apple Photos and Recall send users to a location, and Elastic's semantic highlighter returns passages rather than marked words (Medium). Similarity numbers are model-dependent and uncalibrated, so a percentage would overstate certainty (Low–Medium). Baymard finds dead-end no-results pages lose users and tips alone don't help (Medium). Campus Recall's excerpt plus page/time chip is already right; the no-match page needs a recovery action, and any recent searches should stay on the device and be easy to clear.

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Inline, explicit, sized download on Search, then run the student's query automatically.** Highest impact: it removes the dead end before the core experience (F1, F12). Evidence: Chrome for Developers, web.dev, NN/g (High).
2. **Navigation basics: focus the h1, set a title per screen, make Back close the viewer, and clear stale errors.** Cheap, and removes disorientation on every screen (F3, F4, F6). Evidence: BBC GEL, Deque, Baymard, NN/g (High); GOV.UK on clearing banners (Medium).
3. **Plain-language status and errors, with feedback for the actions that are silent today** (sign out, downloads, cloud delete), plus a "Waiting for search setup" reason on queued items (F2, F3). Evidence: GOV.UK, NN/g, Apple HIG (High). NN/g asks for feedback on every action, Apple HIG and Primer only on significant ones; each silent action today changes data or leaves the device, so all count as significant.
4. **Keeping data on the phone: automatic persistence after the first save, an iPhone Home Screen tip before the download, an Android share target, and the manifest colour fix** (F7, F10, F11). Evidence: WebKit, web.dev, Chrome docs (High); StatCounter India (High).
5. **Search polish: recovery actions on no match, coarse "strong / related" wording instead of numbers, and a short on-device recent-searches list with Clear** (F8). Evidence: Baymard (Medium); recent-search practice rests mostly on Low-tier how-to sources, so treat it as optional.

## Risks and Uncertainties

- **Data gaps:** no 2022–2026 measurement of drop-off for an optional download of about 445 MB (the figures are old Google Play and Poki data); no controlled study of passage vs term highlighting for semantic search; nothing on recent-search privacy expectations.
- **Low-confidence claims:** that iOS keeps Home Screen app storage separate from Safari rests on a 2018 WebKit bug and one web.dev mention. If it holds, models downloaded in a Safari tab must be downloaded again after installing, which is why the Home Screen tip should come before the download. iOS 26 theme-colour behaviour comes from forum and compat-data sources only.
- **Unresolved conflicts:** where focus goes after a route change (h1 vs live-region announcement); `CloseWatcher` availability in Chrome (120 vs 126); confirming every action vs only significant ones; Safari's storage quota (web.dev's ~1 GB prompt vs WebKit's 60% of disk).
- **Product constraints:** S3 makes local delete immediate and permanent, so the trash/undo pattern of comparable apps (Apple Notes 30 days, Keep 7 days) would need a spec change. Existing tests pin the welcome dialog after the landing page, the filter chips on an empty library, and several labels. NN/g's onboarding research argues against the dialog, but removing it is a spec and test decision.

## Next Steps

- Test with 5 students on their own phones (Android-heavy, at least one iPhone): save, find by meaning, open the exact page, turn on sync. Measure task success and the System Usability Scale.
- Decisions this research supports: whether to keep the welcome dialog; whether to add a short "Recently deleted" window (a change to S3); whether to split the model download into a small text pack first and vision, speech and OCR packs later (Play Asset Delivery and Apple Background Assets patterns); whether to offer a clearly labelled title-only search before setup.
- Re-check fast-moving browser support facts (`CloseWatcher`, iOS 26 install and theme behaviour) before the pilot ends.
